# Competitor Pricing and Gating: Gamified Learning Apps and Blackjack Training Products (researched 2026-11-24)

Legend: **[OFFICIAL]** = from the company's own page or app store listing. **[3P]** = third-party review or aggregator (treat as an estimate). **[UNVERIFIED]** = sources conflict or only one weak source. All prices are US dollars. Dates are when the source says it checked the price, where it says so.

## Q1. Freemium models of comparable gamified learning apps (free vs paid, gating type, tiers, prices, trials, lifetime)

### Takeaway
Most comparable apps price at roughly $7 to $18 a month, or $40 to $110 a year, and often offer a lifetime plan at 2 to 4 times the annual price. They use three kinds of gating:
- **Content gating:** Babbel and Brilliant leave only the first lesson or chapter free.
- **Usage limits:** Chess.com caps daily puzzles and lessons, Mimo uses hearts, and Drops caps study time per day.
- **Feature gating:** Busuu, Photomath, Elevate and Chess.com's upper tiers lock analysis and AI tools.

A 7-day free trial is the norm. Chess.com is the closest analogue to a skill-drill app: its free tier uses daily caps, then it has three paid tiers at $49.99, $79.99 and $119.99 a year.

### Inferences
- **Price clusters:**
  - Annual: $40 to $80 for skill and drill apps (Chess.com Gold $49.99, Elevate $39.99, Photomath $59.99, Sololearn $59.99 to $79.99, Mimo about $79.99). Language and curriculum apps run $60 to $110 (Memrise, Busuu, Babbel). Brilliant sits highest at $160 to $240.
  - Monthly: usually $7 to $18. The monthly price is set so annual works out to a 40 to 70% discount, which pushes users toward annual.
- **Lifetime plans:** Babbel, Memrise, Brilliant and possibly Elevate offer one, usually at 2.5 to 4.5 times the annual price and often discounted.
- **Chess.com as a template:** Its Gold, Platinum, Diamond ladder (usage unlock, then analysis unlock, then AI coaching unlock) maps well onto a blackjack drill app. A first tier could unlock unlimited drills; a second could add analytics or mistake review; a third could add deviation and true-count advanced modules or AI explanations.

## Q2. Industry benchmarks for subscription apps (conversion, annual mix, hard vs soft paywall, trials, intro offers)

### Takeaway
RevenueCat's 2026 data (115k+ apps) shows two main results:
- **Hard paywalls vs freemium:** Hard paywalls convert about 5 times better by Day 35 (10.7% vs 2.1% median) and earn about 8 times more revenue per install by Day 60, with no meaningful difference in yearly retention.
- **Education:** The category has the highest median annual price ($44.99) and a $9.99 median monthly price. It leans toward annual plans (about 59 to 66%) and weak annual renewals (about 24%). Its users convert later than other categories, so longer trials (5 to 9 days or more) fit it.

### Cited Findings
- **RevenueCat 2026 scope:** 115,000+ apps, $16B+ revenue, data mostly from 2025. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)
- **Hard paywall vs freemium, Day 35 (2026):** Hard paywall median is 10.7%, down from 12.1% in 2025. Freemium is 2.1%, unchanged.
  - Revenue per install at Day 60 is $3.09 for hard paywalls vs $0.38 for freemium.
  - Yearly-subscriber retention after one year is 27% for hard paywalls vs 28% for freemium, which the report calls "statistically negligible."

  — [RevenueCat blog, 2026-03-19](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- **Hard paywall vs freemium (2025 edition):** 12.11% vs 2.18% median conversion. Refund rates are higher for hard paywalls (5.8% vs 3.4%). — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [RocketShip HQ summary](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/revenuecat-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-summary/)
- **Trial length (2026):**
  - Trials of 17 to 32 days convert at a 42.5% median, against 25.5% for trials under 4 days.
  - 46.5% of apps now use trials of 4 days or less.
  - On 3-day trials, 55.4% of cancellations happen on Day 0.

  — [RevenueCat blog 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- **Annual churn (2026):** About 72% of annual subscribers cancel in year 1, up from about 56%. Month 1 accounts for 35% of all annual cancellations. — [RevenueCat blog 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- **Involuntary churn (2026):** Billing failures cause 31% of cancellations on Google Play and 14% on the App Store. — [RevenueCat blog 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- **Education category, 2026 [OFFICIAL]:**
  - Download-to-trial (D30) median is 6.5%.
  - 78.5% of trial starts happen on Day 0.
  - Only 28.5% of paid conversions happen on Day 0, the lowest of any category.
  - 50.3% of trials are 5 to 9 days long.
  - Median prices are $9.99 monthly and $44.99 yearly; the yearly median is the highest of any category.
  - D14 revenue per install median is $0.30.
  - Median yearly realized LTV per payer is $22.82.
  - Education favors annual plans (59 to 66%, grouped with Travel and Shopping).

  — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)
- **Education annual renewal (2026):** About 24%, among the weakest categories. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 (via search summary)](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)
- **Education in the 2025 edition:**
  - Refund rate is 4.86%, the highest of any category.
  - Over 80% of trials are 5 to 9 days or longer.
  - Revenue per install is $0.40 at the median and $3.13 at the 90th percentile.
  - RevenueCat recommends that Education apps use free trials, yearly prices of $99.99 and up, and consider hard paywalls.

  — [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025); [Singular summary](https://www.singular.net/blog/subscription-apps/)
- **Education trial conversion [UNVERIFIED]:** A secondary blog attributes a 42% Education trial-conversion rate to RevenueCat 2025. This could not be confirmed on RevenueCat's own page. — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/hard-paywall-vs-freemium-2026)
- **Adapty 2026, all categories:** Install-to-trial is 27.8%, trial-to-paid is 59.2%, and first renewal is 45.1%. Adapty says trials boost LTV for annual plans in Education. These figures are far higher than RevenueCat's because the sample and definitions differ (Adapty's customer base is skewed toward performance-marketing apps), so treat them as an upper bound. — [Adapty SOSA 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-report/); [Adapty 2025 summary](https://adapty.io/blog/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2025-in-10-minutes/)

### Inferences
- **Realistic baseline for a small education app:**
  - About 6 to 7% of downloads start a trial.
  - About 2% of downloads pay by Day 35 with a freemium model, or about 10 to 12% of payers-to-installs with a hard paywall. The hard-paywall figure is for apps that choose one, and those apps are likely ones with strong intent traffic.
  - Median revenue per install is about $0.30 to $0.40.
- **Fit for blackjack training:** Card-counting learners are high-intent and goal-driven, which resembles the Education and Health profile. That supports a 7-day trial, an annual-first presentation, and possibly a hard or near-hard paywall after a free basic-strategy module.
- **Refunds and Month 1 churn:** Both are high in Education, so the free value should be real enough that payers aren't surprised by what they bought.

### Gaps
- No Education-specific trial-to-paid or Day 35 download-to-paid median was visible on the part of the RevenueCat page that was read (the page was truncated).
- Data on how introductory offers (discounted first period) perform was not found.
- Sensor Tower and data.ai reports were not reached within the tool budget.

## Q3. What blackjack and card-counting training products charge, and user sentiment

### Takeaway
The market is split in two:
- **Premium course and community:** Blackjack Apprenticeship (BJA) charges $397 to $997 for the first year, then $299 a year, with a $3,499 bootcamp.
- **Cheap app-store trainers:** Mostly free with in-app purchases. Examples are about $4 a month, $25 a year or $40 lifetime (Expert Card Counting), or a single paid app (BJA's own iOS app at about £4.99).

That leaves a wide gap between about $40 and about $400 a year. Reliable user-sentiment data (Reddit) was not obtained.

### Inferences
- **Possible middle tier:** A subscription at about $9.99/mo or $39.99 to $59.99/yr would sit between the $25/yr app trainers and BJA's $299/yr renewal. It would also line up with Education category medians ($9.99/mo, $44.99/yr).
- **Lifetime option:** A lifetime plan (for example $79 to $129) could appeal to this audience, many of whom are one-time learners who master Hi-Lo and stop. That suggests annual churn will be high, which matches Education's roughly 24% annual renewal.
- **Gating that competitors use:** Basic strategy free, with deviations (Illustrious 18), true-count drills, unlimited review and advanced analytics locked. Competitors already gate this way, so users will see it as normal.
