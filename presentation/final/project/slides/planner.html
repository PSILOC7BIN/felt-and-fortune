Step 2 · The planner

A transparent, rule-based “AI”

Placement

Self-reported skill only counts when the checks agree. A stage is skipped only if every earlier stage is too.

Focus areas

A missed check, or your hardest skill, adds a review lesson to that unit.

Pace

Daily minutes set lessons per day, a daily XP goal, and a finish date.

Context

Your goal sets a milestone; your games add notes, e.g., online games reshuffle.

Why rules instead of a large language model? Zero cost, no wrong blackjack math, and every decision can be explained.

About 70 seconds. Here’s the brain of it. The planner is an expert system: a set of explicit rules, not a black box. The most important rule is about placement. If you say you’ve counted cards before but you miss the count check, you don’t get skipped ahead; you get extra counting review instead. Skipping is sequential: you only test out of a stage if you’ve also proven every stage before it. A missed check, or the skill you said was hardest, adds a focus-review lesson. Your daily time sets how many lessons a day, a daily XP goal, and a finish date. Your goal places a milestone on the path, and the games you play add notes. For example, most online blackjack reshuffles every hand, so counting won’t help there. I considered using a large language model to write plans. I chose rules because they cost nothing to run, they can’t get blackjack math confidently wrong, and every decision can be explained to the learner.
