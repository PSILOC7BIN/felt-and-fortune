# Cap practice volume, sell depth, pace mastery

Duolingo earns its money from one thing, subscriptions. Hearts, energy, gems and ads are mostly devices for getting people to subscribe. In Q3 2026 subscriptions brought in **87.4% of revenue**, while sales of gems and refills fell to **2.2%** and were still shrinking. Paid subscribers have stayed at roughly **8.5–9% of monthly users** for three years. Over the same period, streaks, leagues and tuned reminders lifted daily engagement (daily users divided by monthly users) from 30% to 42%. Competitors with a drill format, such as Chess.com, Mimo, Elevate and the cheap blackjack trainers, charge **$40–80 a year**. They limit free use either by volume (a daily cap) or by depth (advanced content and analytics). Hard paywalls convert about five times better than freemium, and yearly retention is the same either way. For this app, the evidence supports **one paid plan, not two stacked ones**: a Free tier and **Pro at $9.99/month or $49.99/year**, with a 14-day trial on the annual plan, plus a **$119.99 Lifetime** purchase. Free users get daily **Energy** (25 units) for standalone practice. Lessons never cost energy, and they are paced by mastery checkpoints rather than by a meter. The owner's instinct to sell a subscription that removes limits is right. Two parts of the proposal are not. Locking the seven practice tools behind the paywall would remove the interactive core, which is where learners get hooked, where they learn, and what gets a Capacitor app past Apple's "repackaged website" rule. A separate top tier that unlocks every tool repeats the Duolingo Max experiment, which never passed 9% of subscribers and is being shut down. Compliance pushes the app firmly into **18+ simulated gambling**. It must present itself strictly as a trainer, because Apple bans "card counters," and it should sell no currency at launch, sell nothing random, run no ads, and use cancellation flows that meet ROSCA and the California and New York rules.

## Duolingo earns 87 cents of each dollar from subscriptions

Duolingo's model is a large free audience multiplied by a stable conversion rate. Daily active users grew from **26.9M in Q4 2023 to 64.9M in Q3 2026**. Paid subscribers grew from 6.6M to 13.1M, yet paid users as a share of MAU stayed at 8.5–9.1% across 2025–2026 ([Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm); [Q4 2024 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000156208825000039/q4fy24duolingo12-31x24shar.htm)). Revenue roughly doubled, from **$531M in FY2023 to $1,038M in FY2025**, and the company guides to $1,221M for FY2026 ([Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)). The engagement mechanics raised how often people come back far more than they raised the rate at which people pay. Revenue growth came from audience growth, price increases and the Family and Max upsells.

**The tiers have collapsed back toward a single Super plan.** Duolingo publishes no official US price list. Third-party trackers saw Super at about **$12.99/month or $83.99/year** on US Google Play in September 2026, and one account saw $16.99 and $119.99. Super Family runs about $119.99/year for up to six accounts ([FamilyPro](https://familypro.io/en/blog/duolingo-max-vs-super); [dealnews](https://www.dealnews.com/features/duolingo/cost)). Super removes ads, gives unlimited hearts or energy, and now includes Video Call. Max launched in March 2023 at **$29.99/month or $167.99/year** with GPT-4 features ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/14/duolingo-launches-new-subscription-tier-with-access-to-ai-tutor-powered-by-gpt-4); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/duolingos-max-plan-offers-ai-tutoring-for-30-per-month-203625446.html?src=rss)). It reached only **9% of subscribers** by Q3 2025, and management said it was underperforming its "lofty expectations" ([Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2025/11/06/duolingo-duol-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript)). In November 2026 Duolingo stopped selling Max. It is moving Video Call into Super and converting individual Max subscribers to Super Family at the same price ([Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)). The standard trial is 14 days. In 2026 the company said it was offering *longer* free trials to lift both revenue and daily users ([Q2 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)).

**Energy replaced Hearts because Hearts punished beginners.** Under Hearts, free users had five lives and each mistake cost one. Beginners were **twice as likely to run out mid-lesson** ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-energy/)). Energy, rolled out from May 2025, gives a **25-unit bar**. Each exercise costs one unit whether the answer is right or wrong, and the bar recharges fully in about a day. Learners earn energy back through streaks of correct answers, perfect lessons, adding friends and chests. Reviewing mistakes costs nothing, and a rewarded ad or gems buys a refill ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-energy/); [The Verge mirror](https://thevergetoday.pages.dev/news/665315/duolingo-hearts-energy-system)). Luis von Ahn said it increased bookings and also increased daily users. He also said cutting the bar from 25 to 24 would make more money but cost daily users, and that Duolingo would not ship that trade ([Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2025/11/06/duolingo-duol-q3-2025-earnings-call-transcript)). The mechanism matters. Energy moves the limit from *skill* to *volume*, so beginners rarely hit it, while heavy users, the people most likely to pay, hit it often. Long-time users did push back: an Android Authority writer said they could no longer meaningfully learn for free, and petitions asked for hearts back ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/quitting-duolingo-energy-system-3599842/); [change.org](https://www.change.org/p/restore-the-hearts-feature-in-duolingo-and-remove-energy)). Duolingo has published no exact figure for how much Energy lifted conversion.

**Gems and ads are support functions, not profit centers.** Gems are a soft currency earned from lessons, quests and leagues and spent on items such as a **Streak Freeze (200 gems)**, an energy refill (about 350) and Legendary challenges (100). These third-party prices vary with A/B tests ([Duolingo Guides](https://duolingoguides.com/?p=7381)). Real-money in-app purchase revenue fell 11%, 23% and 38% year over year across Q1–Q3 2026 ([Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026069474/q3fy26duolingo9-30x26share.htm)). That decline fits the company's 2026 push to reduce friction on the free tier, though Duolingo has not tied the two together. Advertising holds flat at about $21M a quarter even as daily users grow 29%. Its main job is to make ad removal a Super benefit. Leadership's rule is explicit: when user growth and monetization conflict, user growth wins, and there is a "strict guardrail" against degrading the free experience, enforced through hundreds of A/B tests a week ([CX Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/duolingo-ceo-monetize-without-degrading-free-user-experience/873877/)).

## Making streaks easier to keep beat every paywall tweak

The strongest retention evidence comes from making the habit *easier* to keep, not harder to break. In late 2019 Duolingo changed the rule so that one lesson keeps a streak, even if the learner misses their chosen XP goal. Reported relative effects were **+3.3% D14 retention, +1% daily active learners, and 40% more learners on 7+ day streaks**. Before the change, nearly 40% of learners who were active two days running without a streak had chosen the "intense" goal ([Duolingo blog, 2020](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). The share of daily users on a 7+ day streak rose from about a third to more than half ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). Smaller tweaks added up. A milestone animation gave +1.7% week-one retention among new learners, and letting users equip two Streak Freezes instead of one gave +0.38% daily active learners ([Duolingo blog, 2022](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). A June 2025 Streak Revival event brought back **15.4M learners' streaks** ([Q2 2025 letter](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/961ce633-3cee-49d0-bd7a-2c63731d45fb)). Peer-reviewed work explains why repair matters. Across seven studies, Silverman and Barasch found that a streak shown as broken lowers later engagement, and that the drop is smaller when repair is possible ([CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)). Streaks travel well beyond Duolingo: Busuu saw **15% more sessions** in the month after adding them ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/duolingo-streak-feature-app-engagement-growth)).

Competition and reminders form the second layer. Weekly leaderboards were Duolingo's single largest documented engagement gain: **+17% learning time and three times as many highly engaged learners**. They match people with similar study habits and time zones ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth); [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-leagues-leaderboards/)). They also invite XP farming, bots and burnout ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/duolingo-app-cheats-hacks-leagues-xp-why-duohacker-1850506482)). Learners with at least one Friend Streak are **22% more likely** to complete their daily lesson ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/product-lessons-friend-streak/)). Duolingo's bandit algorithm for choosing reminder wording added **+0.5% daily users and +2% new-user retention** against a strong baseline. It penalized reused messages with a decay that has a 15-day half-life, because sending the same template again cost about 0.5% of opens ([Yancey & Settles, KDD 2020](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)). In onboarding, letting people finish a lesson before signing up, and softening the signup walls, gave **+8.2% daily users** ([First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/the-tenets-of-a-b-testing-from-duolingos-master-growth-hacker)). On the learning side, half-life-regression spaced repetition raised daily engagement **12%** ([Settles & Meeder, ACL 2016](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/P16-1174)). Blackjack strategy is a finite grid of about 290 hard, soft and pair cells plus index plays, which makes it unusually well suited to scheduling each cell for review.

One Duolingo tactic should not carry over. The Streak Wager lets users bet gems that they will keep a streak, and a secondary source reports it lifted **D7 retention by 14%** ([Lenny's Newsletter via search](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/duolingo-streaks)). In an app whose subject is wagering, a gem bet is the wrong lesson to teach, and it would add yet another simulated-gambling element to the age rating.

Chess.com, Mimo and Drops cap **usage** with daily limits, hearts or minutes. Chess.com's upper tiers, Elevate and Photomath gate **features** such as analysis and AI explanations. Chess.com is the closest analogue to a skill-drill app. It caps the free tier daily, then stacks three tiers, and it is the only rival here that sustains a three-tier ladder.

Industry data sharpens the choice. RevenueCat's 2026 report covers more than 115,000 apps. It finds hard paywalls convert **10.7% of installs by Day 35, against 2.1% for freemium**, and earn **$3.09 against $0.38 per install by Day 60**, with no meaningful difference in yearly retention (27% vs 28%). It also finds that trials of 17–32 days convert at a 42.5% median, compared with 25.5% for trials under four days ([RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). Education apps have medians of **$9.99/month and $44.99/year**, the highest yearly median of any category. They make only 28.5% of conversions on Day 0, the lowest share of any category, which means education users decide slowly. Their median realized LTV per payer is $22.82 ([RevenueCat Education 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education)). Annual renewal in education is only about **24%**, and across categories 35% of annual cancellations happen in month one ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); [RevenueCat 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). Education also had the highest refund rate in 2025, at 4.86% ([RevenueCat 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)).

The blackjack market has a gap in the middle. At the top, Blackjack Apprenticeship charges **$397–$997 for the first year and $299 a year after that**, plus a $3,499 bootcamp ([BJA store](https://store.blackjackapprenticeship.com/products/membership); [CountingEdge](https://www.countingedge.com/blackjack-apprenticeship/)). At the bottom, Expert Card Counting sells Pro for **$3.89/month, $24.99/year or $39.99 lifetime** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6444629083)), and Reckon sells a $99.99 lifetime unlock ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6768187788)). Blackjack Trainer 21 already gates Illustrious 18 deviations, review mode and an EV calculator behind Pro ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6757376628)), so users of these apps are used to seeing advanced counting locked. Nothing credible sits between about $40 and $300 a year. One gap remains: this research found no Reddit or review-based sentiment on trainer apps.

## Store and consumer law make this an 18+ product

### Apple and Google treat a blackjack trainer as simulated gambling

The biggest single risk is Apple guideline 5.3.4: "Illegal gambling aids, including card counters, are not permitted on the App Store" ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). In practice, Apple lists many card-counting *trainers*, including Blackjack Apprenticeship's app, which dates from around 2010 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id388857410)), Reckon and Card Counting Coach. This research found no documented rejection of an education app under 5.3.4 ([Shopapper](https://shopapper.com/fix-apple-gambling-app-rejection-guideline-5-3/)). The workable reading is that "card counters" means tools used *during* live play. Apple has never published a definition, so that reading is an inference. It rules out features that work at a real table, such as a count tracker meant for the casino, camera input, discreet modes or bet prompts on a watch. It also rules out casino links and "beat the casino" marketing. Google Play allows simulated casinos but forbids directing users to real-money casinos. It also bars apps with simulated gambling content from showing gambling ads ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032)).

Apple's July 2025 age-rating overhaul defines **frequent simulated gambling as 18+**. Infrequent simulated gambling is 13+, and Australia applies R18+ ([Apple age ratings](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions)). A trainer that deals hands all day is frequent, so plan for 18+ on iOS and the IARC simulated-gambling descriptor on Google Play.

**Purchases and subscriptions.** Subscriptions and any currency must use Apple in-app purchase or Google Play Billing. Purchased currency on iOS may not expire. Randomized paid items require odds to be disclosed before purchase on both stores ([Apple 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Google Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738)). In the **US storefront only**, iOS apps may link to web checkout. Apple currently takes **0% on linked-out purchases** while a fee is litigated. Apple has proposed 15%, 10% for renewals and 5% for small businesses ([Mac Observer](https://www.macobserver.com/news/apple-epic-link-out-commission-proposal-15-percent/)), and the Supreme Court hears the case on Jan 11, 2027 ([Oyez](https://www.oyez.org/cases/2026/25-1311)). Google's US alternative-billing and external-link programs have charged service fees since Oct 1, 2026 ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165?hl=en)). Apple's Small Business Program charges 15% on in-app purchases ([Apple SBP](https://developer-mdn.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)).

On iOS, subscriptions must disclose what the user gets and must include a restore mechanism, and an app that creates accounts must offer account deletion inside the app ([Apple Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Google requires terms to be visible without extra taps, bans prices that obscure an annual charge, requires trial-conversion disclosure and a cancellation link, and requires a web URL where users can delete their account ([Google Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533); [Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en-GB)). Marketing push notifications need explicit in-app opt-in and an in-app opt-out ([Apple 4.5.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)).

**Capacitor and guideline 4.2.** Guideline 4.2 rejects apps that feel like a repackaged website. Developers report Capacitor apps rejected even when they had push notifications and sharing ([Ionic Forum](https://forum.ionicframework.com/t/app-store-rejection-4-2-design-minimum-functionality-my-first-after-2-years-of-ionic/200908); [Apple Dev Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/94920)). The strongest defense this app has is its interactive simulator and drills. That is a central reason not to hide them from free users, and from reviewers.

### US consumer law and the FTC's settlements set the paywall's shape

The FTC's 2024 Click-to-Cancel amendments were **vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025**. As of the latest sources found (March–April 2026), the replacement was still at the advance-notice stage, with no proposed rule text ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ftc-relaunches-negative-option-rulemaking-with-new-questions-new-branding)). What binds now is the federal Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (ROSCA), which requires clear disclosure of material terms *before* billing details are collected, express informed consent, and a simple way to cancel. State laws add to it:

- **California** (from July 2025): express affirmative consent; coverage of free-to-paid conversions; annual-renewal reminders; online cancellation; a limit of one save offer during cancellation ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-06-04-california-automatic-renewal-law-amendments-take-effect-on-july-1-2025); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2024/09/california-amends-autorenewal-law-with-stricter)).
- **New York** (from November 2025): cancellation as simple as sign-up; a notice 3–21 days before the deadline for trials longer than a month; 5–30 days' notice of price increases ([Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/ny-quietly-amends-automatic-renewal-law)).
- **New York City**: its own rule took effect Oct 1, 2026 ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/09/new-york-citys-click-to-cancel-rule)).

Virtual currency has its own enforcement history. The FTC's **$20M HoYoverse settlement** (Genshin Impact) targeted multi-layered currency exchange that hid real costs, and misleading loot-box odds. The order required odds and exchange-rate disclosure, direct dollar pricing for items sold for currency, and parental consent before under-16s buy loot boxes ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse)). Epic paid **$245M in refunds** over dark patterns that caused unintended purchases, plus a $275M COPPA penalty ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2023/03/15/ftc-finalizes-epic-games-245m-penalty-over-tricking-users-heres-how-to-get-your-refund/); [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/fortnite-maker-epic-games-to-settle-ftc-case-pay-520-million-in-penalties-and-refunds)). The FTC's dark-patterns report names hidden trial conversion, drip pricing and hard-to-cancel flows as problems ([FTC report](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf)).

Belgium treats paid loot boxes as illegal gambling. PEGI reportedly assigns a default 16 rating to games with paid random items from June 2026, but both claims come from secondary sources ([Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/lootbox-regulation-2026-game-studios/); [Blask](https://blask.com/knowledge/loot-box/)).

The youth evidence is the reason to take the 18+ rating seriously rather than treat it as a formality. Adolescents who played simulated poker were almost **five times more likely** to move to real-money gambling at follow-up. A later analysis found that only card-based poker predicted monetary gambling ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318152965_Transition_from_playing_with_simulated_gambling_games_to_gambling_with_real_money_a_longitudinal_study_in_adolescence)). A German longitudinal study found that simulated gambling predicted problem gambling one year later ([PMC9517771](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9517771/)). Card games are the closest analogue to blackjack, and whether simulated play causes later gambling is still contested. Separately, the amended **COPPA Rule** has required full compliance since April 22, 2026. COPPA can reach services not aimed at children if they use child-appealing content or know they have under-13 users ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [FTC](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse)).

## The recommended design: free strategy, one Pro plan, energy on drills

### The owner's proposal is half right

| Proposal element | What the evidence says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription as the main revenue | Subscriptions are 87% of Duolingo's revenue, and gems are 2% and falling | **Adopt** |
| Lock the seven practice tools behind a subscription | Duolingo caps *volume*, not features, and keeps core learning free. The tools are built into lessons, so locking them breaks lessons. Drills are the spaced-repetition engine (+12% engagement). Interactive drills are the main defense against guideline 4.2. | **Reject as stated**: meter tools with Energy, and lock only advanced modes |
| Heart or energy limit on lesson tracks | Hearts made beginners twice as likely to run out. A meter on lessons slows the on-ramp, which is exactly where free users decide whether to stay. | **Move the meter to standalone practice**: pace lessons with mastery checkpoints instead |
| Base tier gives unlimited hearts | Unlimited use is the core of Super and Mimo Pro. But "unlimited" access to a finite set of 120 lessons invites binge-and-churn, and education annual renewal is only about 24%. | **Adopt for practice energy, not for skipping the pacing** |
| Top tier unlocks all tools | Max never passed 9% of subscribers and is being sunset. A two-tier split also forces users to compare two plans before they understand either. | **Fold into one Pro plan**, and add a higher tier later only if a costly feature such as an AI coach appears |

### Tiers and prices

| Plan | Price | Trial | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free** | $0 | — | All of Smart Player and Strategy Master (rules, money management, basic strategy for every rule set, responsible-gambling content). Unit 1 of Card Counter and of Advantage Pro. All seven tools in core mode with 25 Energy a day. Streaks, freezes and leagues. |
| **Pro** | **$9.99/month** or **$49.99/year** (shown as $4.17/mo, 58% off monthly) | **14 days on annual**; no trial on monthly | Everything in Free, plus all of Card Counter and Advantage Pro, unlimited Energy, advanced tool modes, a mistake-analytics dashboard, custom table rules, and extra cosmetic slots |
| **Pro Lifetime** | **$119.99** one-time (about 2.4× annual) | — | Pro, forever. Fits learners who master Hi-Lo and stop. |

These prices sit on the education medians of $9.99 and $44.99 and on Chess.com Gold's $49.99. They are about double the $24.99 app trainers and an order of magnitude below Blackjack Apprenticeship. The 14-day trial follows both Duolingo's standard and RevenueCat's finding that education users convert late and that longer trials convert better. Fourteen days is also under New York's one-month threshold for a pre-conversion notice, but send a reminder three days before conversion anyway. Lifetime sits above Reckon's $99.99 because this app has more content. Freemium medians set the expectations: about 2% of installs paying by Day 35, and about **$0.30–0.40 revenue per install**, which is roughly $3,000–4,000 per 10,000 installs by Day 60 (derived from [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)). Selling the web version through Stripe, with a US-only link-out on iOS, is currently commission-free on Apple. Budget for a 5–15% Apple fee arriving later.

The free/Pro line deliberately falls between **losing less** and **advantage play**. Basic strategy, house-edge literacy and responsible-gambling content stay free and unlimited, as the fairness guidance recommends. The parts competitors and Blackjack Apprenticeship already charge for become Pro: Card Counter beyond its first unit, index plays, Kelly betting and team play. This works as a near-hard paywall for the high-intent users who pick the Card Counter or Advantage Pro goal at onboarding. They see their first unit, then the paywall, which captures much of the conversion advantage of a hard paywall. Users who only want to stop making costly mistakes keep a complete, generous product.

### Energy, not hearts, with exact numbers

Use **Energy for free-tier standalone practice**, applied as follows:

- **Capacity and refill:** the bar holds **25**, matching Duolingo's tested number. It refills **1 unit every 58 minutes**, so it is full again in about a day.
- **What costs 1 Energy:** one standalone round. For each tool that means:
  - Strategy Trainer: 10 hands
  - Count Trainer: one deck run
  - Deck Estimator: 5 estimates
  - True Count Trainer: 10 conversions
  - Index Trainer: 10 decisions
  - Bankroll Simulator: one run
- **What costs more:** a full Shoe Challenge costs **3 Energy**.
- **What costs nothing:** lessons, including the tool exercises embedded in them, and mistake-review rounds, as in Duolingo's design.
- **Earning it back:** +1 Energy for a perfect round, and +1 for every 15 correct decisions in a row, capped at +10 a day so the cap stays meaningful.
- **No interruptions:** charge each round when it starts, so a round is never cut off halfway.

Twenty-five rounds is roughly 30–40 minutes of drilling. That is several times a modest daily goal, so casual learners never hit the wall and heavy drillers do. This is the Duolingo pattern that lifted both bookings and daily users. Keep the Shoe Challenge's three hearts as a rule of the challenge itself, like a boss level, and never let money refill them. The fairness guidance prefers charging only for mistakes. That argument is real, but Duolingo's own data shows mistake-based limits hurt the beginners an education app most needs to keep. Free mistake review and accuracy-based earn-back answer most of the concern. Test caps of 20 and 30 against 25, using daily users as the guardrail metric.

Run **no ads**. Google bars gambling ads in simulated-gambling apps, ad networks could serve casino creatives, and advertising is only 7% of Duolingo's revenue even with tens of millions of daily users. Leaving ads out also means there are no rewarded ads, so energy refills come from earned currency only.

### Virtual currency stays earned and separate from chips

Use one learning currency with a non-casino name such as "Stars." Keep it entirely separate from simulator play chips, which should be resettable for free and never purchasable, transferable or redeemable. That separation keeps the app out of social-casino territory, and Apple's simulated-gambling definition depends on currency having no real-money exchange value.

At launch, **do not sell the currency**. Duolingo's own currency revenue is small and falling, iOS currency may not expire once sold, and the HoYoverse order shows the risks of layered currency.

| Earn | Stars | Spend | Stars |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lesson completed | 10 | Streak Freeze (equip up to 2) | 200 |
| Practice round at ≥90% accuracy | 5 | Full Energy refill (free tier) | 350 |
| Daily goal met | 20 | Cosmetics (card backs, felts, avatars) | 100–1,000 |
| Streak milestones 3/7/14/30/100 days | 25/50/100/200/500 (fixed, never random) | Simulator chip reset | Free |

An engaged daily learner earns about 40–60 Stars a day: enough for a freeze every four or five days and an occasional refill. Chests, if any, are free, earned and deterministic, so their odds are disclosed by design. If Stars are ever sold, sell them in a single currency, print the dollar price beside every Stars price, offer the same items for dollars directly, and never sell XP boosts that count toward league rank.

### The retention loop rewards accuracy, not volume

The daily layer:

- **Streaks:** one practice round or one lesson keeps the streak alive, separate from the daily goal, which defaults to the lowest option.
- **Freezes:** grant one free freeze at day 3 and another at day 7, and celebrate milestones at 3, 7, 14, 30 and 100 days.
- **Repair:** offer a free repair within 48 hours, once a month. A free repair, not shame, is what the research shows preserves later engagement.
- **XP:** weight it by decision difficulty and correctness. A soft-total split decision or a true-count index play earns more than a hard 17 stand, so farming easy hands pays little.
- **Spaced review:** schedule a review queue for every strategy cell and index play, so drills always surface the cells each learner is forgetting.

The weekly layer:

- **Leagues:** rank on accuracy-weighted XP within cohorts of similar study habits, or among friends until the user base can fill real leagues. Remove cheaters and offer an opt-out.
- **Social:** add Friend Streaks, capped at a handful.

Reminders:

- Write 10–15 template variants, selected by a simple bandit that rewards an app open within two hours. Never repeat a template within about two weeks, and time reminders to each user's usual practice hour.
- Keep reminders neutral, never guilt-tripping, and put promotions behind a separate opt-in toggle.
- Leave out Streak Wager entirely.

### Pacing turns 120 lessons into six to nine months

Three rules apply to every user, Pro included, because pacing is teaching, not a paywall:

- **New-lesson cap:** at most **2 new lessons a day**.
- **Unit checkpoints:** each of the 8 units per track ends in a checkpoint that unlocks no earlier than the next calendar day. Passing it requires **90% accuracy in the matching tool on two separate days**.
- **Card Counter speed gates:** these checkpoints also require speed benchmarks in the Count Trainer and True Count Trainer, met across several sessions.

Experts can still test out, and units they test out of stay open for review. At the default goal of about one lesson plus reviews a day, a track takes **six to eight weeks**, and the four tracks together take **six to nine months**. The fastest legitimate route is about three weeks per track. These durations come from this design, not from published data. The evidence behind the approach is that spaced practice raises engagement, that Duolingo's "intense" goals hurt streaks, and that education subscribers churn heavily in month one and at renewal. A visible months-long roadmap, with Card Counter and Advantage Pro as the destination, is the best case for the annual plan.

## Conclusion

The central finding is that Duolingo's paywall sits on *volume*, not on *knowledge*, and that its biggest retention gains came from making the habit forgiving. Applied to this app, the owner's proposal is inverted. The seven tools are the product: they are where skill is built, what gets the app past Apple's review, and the experience that persuades someone to pay. They should be available to everyone, with the meter on them. What should be scarce is advantage-play depth: counting, index plays, Kelly betting and team play. A blackjack trainer also has an asset Duolingo lacks. Its natural premium content is exactly what its high-intent buyers already expect to pay for, so the conversion advantage of a hard paywall is available at the Card Counter checkpoint without walling off the free basic-strategy education that keeps the product honest.

The second implication is that in a gambling-adjacent app, ethics and store approval point the same way. Every mechanic this report removes would raise review risk and the age-rating question together while adding little revenue: sold currency, paid chests, gem wagers, casino ads and chip top-ups. The open questions to test first are the energy cap (20, 25 or 30) measured against daily users, the length of the annual trial, and whether the $119.99 Lifetime plan pulls buyers away from the annual plan or adds new ones. The research also leaves gaps: no exact figure for Energy's conversion lift, no user sentiment on trainer apps, and a pending decision on Apple's link-out fee.
